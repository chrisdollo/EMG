WHAT WAS DONE
-------------
Trained three CNN/TCN architectures on the putEMG dataset using full
Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation across all 44 subjects.

Each fold:
  - Test    : 1 held-out subject (never seen during training)
  - Train   : 90% of remaining 43 subjects' data (stratified by class)
  - Val     : 10% of remaining 43 subjects' data (early stopping only)

Best-epoch weights are saved per fold and checkpointing allows resuming
mid-run. All three models ran to completion (44/44 folds each).

Training configuration:
  Optimizer   : Adam (lr=1e-3)
  LR schedule : ReduceLROnPlateau (factor=0.5, patience=5, min_lr=1e-6)
  Early stop  : patience=5, min_delta=0.002
  Max epochs  : 20
  Dropout     : 0.1
  Batch size  : 16
  Input shape : (1, 24, 1500) — 24 sEMG channels, 1500 samples per rep

KEY OBSERVATIONS
----------------

1. EMG_TCN is the best generalizing architecture across subjects.
   Dilated temporal convolutions appear well-suited to cross-subject sEMG.

2. All three models show high variance (~12 pp std). Subject-specific
   signal characteristics drive large differences: some subjects are
   classified near-perfectly (>98%) while others are near-chance (<50%).

3. Subject 06 is the hardest for both EMG_TCN and EEGNet. Subject 27
   is the easiest, scoring 97.50% (EMG_TCN), 98.21% (EEGNet), 95.00%
   (ShallowConvNet).

4. Val accuracy is consistently higher than test accuracy (~93% vs ~80%
   for EMG_TCN). The gap reflects how poorly inter-subject variation
   is captured by the training pool — the model sees consistent val
   performance but cannot predict the new subject's style.

5. No model reaches the ~90% within-subject benchmark (putEMG paper
   SVM+RMS), confirming that cross-subject generalization is fundamentally
   harder. Subject-adaptive fine-tuning is the most promising next step.
